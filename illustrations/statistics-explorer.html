What each one means

Mean. Add up all the values and share the total out equally: total ÷ how many. On the picture it is the balance point. Think of the number line as a seesaw and the dots as equal weights: it balances on the yellow triangle under the line. A dot far away has a big pull, so one unusual value can drag the mean a long way.

Median. Put the values in order and take the middle one. If there are two middle ones, take halfway between them. It is marked by the purple triangle: half the dots are on each side of it. Moving the biggest dot even further away doesn't change the median at all.

Mode. The most common value: the tallest stack of dots. There can be two or more modes, or none at all if every value appears the same number of times.

Range. Largest value minus smallest value. Quick to find, but it only looks at the two end dots, so one unusual value can make it huge.

Standard deviation (SD). Roughly, how far a typical dot is from the mean. A small SD means the dots huddle close to the mean; a big SD means they are spread out. Each yellow line on the picture is one dot's distance from the mean, and the SD is a kind of average of those lengths. It gives far-away dots a bit more weight, so it is never smaller than the plain average distance. In many data sets about two thirds of the dots lie within 1 SD of the mean: the yellow band.

Adding the same number to every value moves the whole picture sideways, so the SD stays the same. Spreading the dots out makes it bigger.

On a calculator the SD is usually labelled σx, and that is the one used here. If it also shows sx, that is a slightly bigger version used when the data is only a sample from a larger group.

Things to try

  • "With an outlier": drag the dot at 20 even further right. The mean and SD change; the median doesn't.
  • Switch between "Same mean, small spread" and "Same mean, big spread". The mean is 10 both times, but look at the yellow band.
  • Press "Add 1 to all" a few times. What happens to the SD?
  • Make the SD as small as possible. What do the dots have to look like?
  • Build a data set where the mean, median and mode are all different.
